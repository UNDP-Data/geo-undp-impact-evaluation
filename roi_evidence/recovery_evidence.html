Seeing Recovery from Space

Satellite Evidence on Early Recovery Impact in Crisis Countries

UNDP Crisis Bureau / C3RT

The question

UNDP invests billions in early recovery.

Can we measure what those investments achieve, quickly and credibly?

We can now track economic recovery from space, using satellite nighttime lights as an independent proxy for economic activity.

No surveys. No self-reported numbers. Near-real-time. Verifiable.

One headline you can stand behind

Myanmar, 2025 earthquake

Areas where UNDP worked recovered 30–55% more than comparable areas.

Measured with a difference-in-differences model — comparing the same areas before and after the earthquake, and comparing UNDP areas against similar non-UNDP areas.

Statistically significant (p < 0.001), robust across multiple distance bands. This is a credible causal estimate.

Gaza: the method shows what works

Among all UNDP intervention types, only employment / cash-for-work shows a measurable positive economic signal.

Intervention Economic signal (nightlights)
Employment / cash-for-work +11.8% → significant
Debris removal negative
Shelter none detected
Water & sanitation none detected

Debris and shelter show physical reconstruction in radar — but not yet economic activity. The method honestly detects where recovery has, and has not, taken hold.

Why this matters

  • Fast. Recovery visible within months, from space — no waiting for surveys.
  • Independent. Satellite data can validate self-reported beneficiary numbers.
  • Scalable. 140+ countries pre-configured; a new country in under an hour.
  • Low cost. A fraction of a traditional impact evaluation.

What it would take to prove impact everywhere

Today, only Myanmar carries a rigorous causal estimate.

The same difference-in-differences method can be extended to the other countries wherever we have project locations — turning “recovery happened alongside UNDP” into “UNDP caused additional recovery.”

That is the natural next step: from monitoring to attribution.

Methodology & Assumptions

How the estimates are built

Recovery: share of crisis-driven decline in nighttime lights that has been regained (pre-crisis baseline → trough → today).

GDP conversion: nightlight change × 0.3 elasticity (Henderson, Storeygard & Weil, 2012 — a global average).

Confidence tiers:

  • Causal — difference-in-differences vs matched controls (Myanmar)
  • Sector — significant sector-level effect (Gaza employment)
  • Correlational — pre-post recovery during UNDP engagement (others)

Limitations (read before citing)

  • Recovery in an affected area reflects all actors — government, agencies, private sector, communities — not UNDP alone. Only Myanmar isolates UNDP’s marginal effect.
  • The 0.3 elasticity is a global average; least reliable in very low-light economies (Syria, Afghanistan) and active-conflict settings where lights reflect non-economic activity (Ukraine, DR Congo).
  • Budgets are allocations, not disbursements.
  • Indicative dollar values are available in the technical annex, framed as all-actor economic activity, not attributable returns.
  • This is a rapid-screening tool, not a substitute for full impact evaluation.

Bottom line

We can now see post-crisis economic recovery from space.

In Myanmar, UNDP’s presence is credibly linked to 30–55% faster recovery.

In Gaza, the method pinpoints employment as what is working.

The next step is causal testing across more countries — to turn monitoring into proven impact.
